General warning signs
- Unexplained weight loss or weight change can be a warning sign.
- Fatigue that does not improve with rest can be concerning.
- A new lump or swelling should be checked.
- Unusual bleeding or discharge can be a warning sign.
- A sore that does not heal needs evaluation.
- Changes in bowel or bladder habits can matter.
- Persistent cough, hoarseness, or trouble swallowing should not be ignored.
- Skin changes, including a changing mole or new skin lesion, can be important.
- These symptoms do not always mean cancer.
- The key concern is whether the symptom is new, persistent, or getting worse.

Common symptoms by cancer type
- Lung cancer may cause persistent cough, chest pain, shortness of breath, coughing up blood, frequent lung infections, or hoarseness.
- Breast cancer may cause a lump in the breast or underarm, changes in breast shape or size, nipple discharge, or skin dimpling and redness.
- Colorectal cancer may cause blood in stool, persistent diarrhea or constipation, abdominal pain or bloating, unexplained weight loss, or changes in bowel habits.
- Prostate cancer may cause difficulty urinating, frequent urination, blood in urine or semen, pelvic discomfort, a weak urine stream, trouble starting urination, or pain when urinating.
- Skin cancer may cause new or changing moles, irregular borders, uneven color, sores that do not heal, or unusual skin color changes.
- Cervical or endometrial cancer may cause abnormal vaginal bleeding, pelvic pain, pain during intercourse, unusual vaginal discharge, or bleeding after menopause.
- Ovarian cancer may cause bloating, pelvic or abdominal pain, feeling full quickly, appetite changes, or urinary changes.
- Pancreatic cancer may cause upper abdominal pain, unexplained weight loss, poor appetite, new diabetes, or jaundice.
- Liver cancer may cause jaundice, abdominal swelling or pain, loss of appetite, fatigue, or weakness.
- Stomach cancer may cause persistent indigestion, loss of appetite, stomach pain, nausea or vomiting, or feeling full quickly after eating.
- Lymphoma or leukemia may cause swollen lymph nodes, fever, night sweats, fatigue, frequent infections, easy bruising, or unexplained weight loss.

Early detection importance
- Early detection matters because cancer found sooner is usually smaller and easier to treat.
- Treatment is often more effective when cancer is still at an early stage.
- Early treatment can offer more options, including local treatment such as surgery.
- Early detection can reduce treatment intensity, side effects, and long-term complications.
- Early detection can improve survival and quality of life.
- Screening can find some cancers before symptoms appear.
- New symptoms should also be checked early because some cancers do not cause obvious symptoms until they are advanced.

When to seek medical attention
- Seek medical attention for a new lump or swelling.
- Seek medical attention for unexplained weight loss.
- Seek medical attention for persistent cough, hoarseness, or trouble swallowing.
- Seek medical attention for unusual bleeding or discharge.
- Seek medical attention for a sore that does not heal.
- Seek medical attention for changes in bowel or bladder habits.
- Seek medical attention for ongoing pain without a clear cause.
- Seek medical attention for fever, night sweats, or major fatigue that does not improve.
- Seek medical attention sooner if a symptom is affecting daily life, returning repeatedly, or clearly worsening.
- People with higher cancer risk should pay particular attention to screening and checkups.

Symptom variations by stage
- Early stage cancers may cause no symptoms or only subtle local signs.
- Middle stage cancers are more likely to cause pain, swelling, cough, hoarseness, trouble swallowing, or enlarged lymph nodes.
- Advanced stage cancers are more likely to cause fatigue, weight loss, poor appetite, persistent pain, and symptoms from distant spread such as shortness of breath, jaundice, bone pain, headaches, or seizures.
