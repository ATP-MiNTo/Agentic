Overview
- Cancer is a group of diseases in which abnormal cells grow uncontrollably.
- These cells can invade nearby tissues and spread to other parts of the body through the blood or lymph system.
- The spread of cancer to distant organs is called metastasis, and it is a major cause of cancer-related death.

How cancer develops
- Cancer often starts with changes in a cell's DNA.
- DNA changes can happen because of errors during cell division, harmful exposures such as tobacco smoke or radiation, inherited mutations, infections, and environmental or lifestyle factors.
- As abnormal cells continue to divide, they may form tumors and spread through the bloodstream or lymphatic system.

Common types
- There are more than 100 types of cancer.
- Carcinoma begins in epithelial cells and includes lung, breast, and colorectal cancer.
- Sarcoma develops in bones, muscles, fat, or connective tissues.
- Leukemia affects blood-forming tissues such as the bone marrow and blood cells.
- Lymphoma affects the lymphatic system and immune cells.
- Melanoma develops in pigment-producing cells called melanocytes.

Cancer statistics
- In 2022, there were about 20 million new cancer cases and 9.7 million cancer deaths worldwide.
- Lung cancer was the most commonly diagnosed cancer in 2022 and the leading cause of cancer death worldwide.
- Other common cancers include breast, colorectal, prostate, and stomach cancer.
- Cancer remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide.
- About 400,000 children develop cancer each year worldwide.

Main risk factors
- Cancer risk factors increase the chance of developing cancer, but they do not guarantee that a person will get cancer.
- Risk rises with age.
- Tobacco use strongly increases cancer risk.
- Heavy alcohol use raises the risk of several cancers.
- Family history and inherited gene changes can increase risk.
- Excess body weight and low physical activity increase the risk of several cancers.
- UV exposure from sunlight or tanning beds increases skin cancer risk.
- Infections such as HPV can increase cancer risk.
- Environmental exposures such as radon, asbestos, secondhand smoke, and certain chemicals can raise risk.

Prevention strategies
- Avoid tobacco and secondhand smoke.
- Maintain a healthy weight.
- Be physically active.
- Eat a healthy diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and beans.
- Limit alcohol.
- Protect yourself from the sun.
- Get vaccinated against HPV and hepatitis B when appropriate.
- Use screening tests such as mammograms, Pap tests, colonoscopy, and low-dose CT when recommended.

# Additional Information
Cancer is one of the most serious health problems worldwide and remains a leading cause of death in many countries. Cancer is not a single disease but a group of diseases characterized by the uncontrolled growth and division of abnormal cells in the body. These abnormal cells can invade nearby tissues and spread to other organs through the blood or lymphatic system, a process known as metastasis. Despite major advances in medical science, it is still not always possible to determine exactly why one person develops cancer while another does not. However, scientific research has shown that certain “risk factors” can significantly increase the likelihood of developing cancer.
Cancer risk factors refer to anything that increases a person’s chance of developing cancer. These factors may come from environmental exposures, lifestyle behaviors, aging, inherited genetic mutations, or exposure to cancer-causing substances known as carcinogens. In contrast, there are also “protective factors” that may help lower the risk of cancer, such as regular physical activity, healthy eating habits, and avoiding harmful substances. While some cancer risk factors can be controlled or avoided, others, such as age and family history, cannot be changed.
One of the most important risk factors for cancer is advancing age. As people grow older, their cells accumulate more DNA damage and genetic mutations over time. At the same time, the body’s ability to repair damaged cells and maintain an effective immune response gradually declines. As a result, older individuals have a higher chance of developing cancer than younger people. Medical statistics show that the average age of diagnosis for many cancers is in older adulthood. For example, the median age at diagnosis is around 63 years for breast cancer, 66 years for colorectal cancer, and 71 years for lung cancer. Nevertheless, cancer can occur at any age, including in children and adolescents, especially certain cancers such as bone cancer and some brain cancers.
Alcohol consumption is another major risk factor associated with cancer. Drinking alcohol, especially in large amounts and over long periods, can damage body tissues, increase inflammation, and interfere with the body’s ability to repair DNA. Alcohol consumption has been linked to cancers of the mouth, throat, esophagus, larynx, liver, and breast. The risk becomes even greater when alcohol use is combined with tobacco use. Although red wine has sometimes been believed to have anticancer properties because it contains antioxidants such as resveratrol, there is currently no clear scientific evidence that drinking red wine reduces cancer risk.
Environmental carcinogens also play a significant role in cancer development. Many chemicals and pollutants can damage DNA and trigger abnormal cellular changes. Examples include tobacco smoke, arsenic, asbestos, industrial chemicals, and air pollution. The level of cancer risk depends on factors such as the amount of exposure, the duration of exposure, and an individual’s genetic background. People who work in industrial settings or live in highly polluted environments for long periods may face a greater risk of developing certain cancers.
Chronic inflammation is another important contributor to cancer development. Inflammation is normally a protective response that helps the body heal from injury or infection. However, when inflammation becomes chronic and persists over time, it can damage tissues and DNA, increasing the likelihood of cancerous changes in cells. For example, people with chronic inflammatory bowel diseases such as ulcerative colitis or Crohn’s disease have an increased risk of colorectal cancer. Obesity is also associated with low-grade chronic inflammation, which may promote cancer development.
Diet and nutrition are closely linked to cancer risk as well. Consuming highly processed foods, foods high in unhealthy fats, or meat cooked at very high temperatures until charred may increase cancer risk because these foods can contain carcinogenic compounds such as HCAs and PAHs. Researchers have also studied substances such as acrylamide, artificial sweeteners, antioxidants, vitamin D, and cruciferous vegetables for their possible relationship to cancer. However, in many cases, scientific evidence is still insufficient to conclusively determine whether these substances directly cause or prevent cancer in humans.
Hormones, particularly estrogen, are associated with the risk of certain cancers such as breast cancer and endometrial cancer. Women who begin menstruation early, experience menopause late, have their first pregnancy at an older age, or never give birth may have a higher risk because they are exposed to estrogen for longer periods throughout their lives.
A weakened immune system is another important cancer risk factor. Individuals with compromised immune systems, such as people living with HIV or organ transplant recipients taking immunosuppressive drugs, are at greater risk of developing various cancers. This is because their immune systems are less capable of detecting and destroying abnormal cells or controlling infections associated with cancer.
Certain infections caused by viruses, bacteria, and parasites are also strongly linked to cancer development. Human papillomavirus (HPV) is the primary cause of cervical cancer, while hepatitis B and hepatitis C viruses increase the risk of liver cancer. Helicobacter pylori infection is associated with stomach cancer. These infectious agents may interfere with normal cell regulation, weaken the immune system, or cause chronic inflammation that contributes to cancer formation.
Obesity and physical inactivity are also major cancer risk factors. People with obesity have a higher risk of developing several cancers, including breast cancer, colorectal cancer, endometrial cancer, pancreatic cancer, and kidney cancer. Obesity can disrupt hormone balance, increase inflammation, and alter metabolism, all of which may contribute to cancer development. Regular exercise and maintaining a healthy body weight can help reduce these risks.
Radiation exposure is another factor that can lead to cancer. Ionizing radiation, such as X-rays, gamma rays, and radon gas, has enough energy to damage DNA and increase the risk of cancer. Ultraviolet (UV) radiation from sunlight and tanning beds is strongly associated with skin cancer. Protecting the skin by using sunscreen, wearing protective clothing, and avoiding excessive sun exposure are important preventive measures.
Finally, tobacco use is considered one of the leading and most preventable causes of cancer worldwide. Tobacco smoke contains numerous harmful chemicals that damage DNA and promote cancer formation. Smoking increases the risk of many cancers, including lung cancer, oral cancer, esophageal cancer, liver cancer, pancreatic cancer, bladder cancer, and colorectal cancer. Secondhand smoke also increases cancer risk. There is no safe level of tobacco use, and quitting smoking significantly reduces the risk of cancer and improves overall health and life expectancy.
In conclusion, cancer is a complex disease caused by the interaction of multiple factors, including age, genetics, environmental exposures, infections, and lifestyle behaviors. Although some risk factors cannot be avoided, many others can be reduced through healthy lifestyle choices such as avoiding tobacco and excessive alcohol consumption, eating a balanced diet, exercising regularly, maintaining a healthy weight, protecting against harmful radiation, and reducing exposure to carcinogens. Increasing public awareness and understanding of cancer risk factors is essential for cancer prevention and for promoting long-term health and well-being.
