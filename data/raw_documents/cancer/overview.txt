Overview
- Cancer is a group of diseases in which abnormal cells grow uncontrollably.
- These cells can invade nearby tissues and spread to other parts of the body through the blood or lymph system.
- The spread of cancer to distant organs is called metastasis, and it is a major cause of cancer-related death.

How cancer develops
- Cancer often starts with changes in a cell's DNA.
- DNA changes can happen because of errors during cell division, harmful exposures such as tobacco smoke or radiation, inherited mutations, infections, and environmental or lifestyle factors.
- As abnormal cells continue to divide, they may form tumors and spread through the bloodstream or lymphatic system.

Common types
- There are more than 100 types of cancer.
- Carcinoma begins in epithelial cells and includes lung, breast, and colorectal cancer.
- Sarcoma develops in bones, muscles, fat, or connective tissues.
- Leukemia affects blood-forming tissues such as the bone marrow and blood cells.
- Lymphoma affects the lymphatic system and immune cells.
- Melanoma develops in pigment-producing cells called melanocytes.

Cancer statistics
- In 2022, there were about 20 million new cancer cases and 9.7 million cancer deaths worldwide.
- Lung cancer was the most commonly diagnosed cancer in 2022 and the leading cause of cancer death worldwide.
- Other common cancers include breast, colorectal, prostate, and stomach cancer.
- Cancer remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide.
- About 400,000 children develop cancer each year worldwide.

Main risk factors
- Cancer risk factors increase the chance of developing cancer, but they do not guarantee that a person will get cancer.
- Risk rises with age.
- Tobacco use strongly increases cancer risk.
- Heavy alcohol use raises the risk of several cancers.
- Family history and inherited gene changes can increase risk.
- Excess body weight and low physical activity increase the risk of several cancers.
- UV exposure from sunlight or tanning beds increases skin cancer risk.
- Infections such as HPV can increase cancer risk.
- Environmental exposures such as radon, asbestos, secondhand smoke, and certain chemicals can raise risk.

Prevention strategies
- Avoid tobacco and secondhand smoke.
- Maintain a healthy weight.
- Be physically active.
- Eat a healthy diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and beans.
- Limit alcohol.
- Protect yourself from the sun.
- Get vaccinated against HPV and hepatitis B when appropriate.
- Use screening tests such as mammograms, Pap tests, colonoscopy, and low-dose CT when recommended.
