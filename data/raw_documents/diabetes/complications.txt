Short-term Complications
- Short-term complications of diabetes can happen suddenly and may become serious if not treated quickly.
- These include hypoglycemia, hyperglycemia, and diabetic ketoacidosis.
- Symptoms may include dizziness, confusion, thirst, frequent urination, and fatigue.
- Proper monitoring of blood glucose levels can help prevent these complications.

Long-term Complications
- Long-term complications develop gradually when blood sugar levels remain uncontrolled for a long period.
- Diabetes can damage blood vessels and organs, leading to heart disease, kidney disease, nerve damage, and eye problems.
- Maintaining healthy blood sugar levels can reduce the risk of these conditions.

Heart Disease and Diabetes
- People with diabetes are more likely to develop cardiovascular diseases such as heart attack and stroke.
- High blood sugar can damage blood vessels and increase blood pressure and cholesterol levels.
- Regular exercise, healthy eating, and medication can help lower the risk of heart disease.

Kidney Disease
- Diabetes is one of the leading causes of kidney disease.
- High blood sugar damages the small blood vessels in the kidneys, reducing their ability to filter waste from the blood.
- Early signs may include swelling, fatigue, and changes in urination.
- Regular kidney function tests are important for people with diabetes.

Neuropathy
- Diabetic neuropathy is nerve damage caused by prolonged high blood sugar levels.
- It often affects the feet and hands, causing numbness, tingling, pain, or weakness.
- Proper foot care and blood sugar control can help prevent severe nerve damage.
