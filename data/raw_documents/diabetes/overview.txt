Definition of Diabetes
- Diabetes is a chronic disease that occurs when the body cannot properly regulate blood sugar levels.
- This happens when the body does not produce enough insulin or cannot effectively use the insulin it produces.
- High blood sugar levels over time can lead to serious health complications affecting many parts of the body.

Type 1 vs Type 2 Differences
- Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks the insulin-producing cells in the pancreas.
- People with Type 1 diabetes usually require insulin therapy for life.
- Type 2 diabetes occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin or does not produce enough insulin.
- Type 2 diabetes is more common and is often linked to lifestyle factors such as obesity and lack of physical activity.

Risk Factors
- Family history can increase the risk of developing diabetes.
- Obesity, unhealthy diet, and physical inactivity are common risk factors.
- High blood pressure and age can also increase risk.
- For Type 1 diabetes, genetics and autoimmune factors may play an important role.

Statistics and Prevalence
- Diabetes affects millions of people worldwide and continues to increase each year.
- Type 2 diabetes accounts for the majority of diabetes cases.
- The growing prevalence of diabetes is associated with changes in lifestyle, aging populations, and increasing obesity rates.

Impact on the Body
- Diabetes can affect many organs and systems in the body.
- High blood sugar levels may damage blood vessels, nerves, kidneys, eyes, and the heart.
- If not properly managed, diabetes can lead to cardiovascular disease, kidney failure, nerve damage, and vision problems.
