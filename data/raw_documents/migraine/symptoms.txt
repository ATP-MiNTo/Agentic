Last Updated: 2026

Early Warning Signs (Prodrome)
- The prodrome phase may begin several hours or up to two days before the headache starts.
- Common signs include mood changes, fatigue, food cravings, neck stiffness, increased thirst, frequent urination, constipation, difficulty concentrating, and excessive yawning.
- Recognizing prodrome symptoms may help a person prepare for or manage an upcoming migraine attack.

Aura Symptoms
- Aura symptoms occur in some people before or during a migraine attack.
- They usually develop gradually over several minutes and last 5 to 60 minutes.
- Common visual symptoms include flashing lights, blind spots, zigzag patterns, and blurred vision.
- Other aura symptoms may include numbness or tingling in the face, arms, or legs, weakness, dizziness, ringing in the ears, and difficulty speaking or concentrating.
- Aura symptoms are temporary and reversible.

Headache Phase Symptoms
- Migraine pain is typically throbbing or pulsating and often affects one side of the head.
- The headache may worsen with physical activity.
- Common symptoms include nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light, sound, smells, or touch.
- Some people also experience dizziness, blurred vision, or extreme fatigue.
- The severity can significantly interfere with daily activities and quality of life.

Post-Headache Phase (Postdrome)
- The postdrome stage is often called a migraine hangover.
- It may last from several hours to up to 48 hours.
- Common symptoms include fatigue, weakness, dizziness, difficulty concentrating, body aches, mood changes, and continued sensitivity to light and sound.
- Some people feel mentally drained or confused, while others may feel temporary relief or mild euphoria.

Typical Symptom Duration
- Prodrome symptoms may begin 24 to 48 hours before the headache.
- Aura symptoms generally last between 5 and 60 minutes.
- The headache phase commonly lasts from 4 to 72 hours if untreated.
- The postdrome phase can continue for several hours or up to two days.
- A full migraine attack may span several days from beginning to recovery.

Symptom Triggers
- Common triggers include emotional stress, hormonal changes, lack of sleep, oversleeping, dehydration, skipped meals, and intense physical activity.
- Environmental triggers may include bright lights, loud noises, strong smells, and weather changes.
- Food and beverage triggers can include alcohol, caffeine, chocolate, aged cheese, processed foods, and additives such as monosodium glutamate.
- Identifying personal triggers may help reduce the frequency of migraine attacks.
