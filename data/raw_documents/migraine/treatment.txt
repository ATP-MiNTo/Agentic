Over-the-counter Medications
- Over-the-counter medications are commonly used to relieve mild to moderate migraine symptoms.
- Common examples include acetaminophen, ibuprofen, and aspirin.
- These medications may help reduce pain and discomfort when taken early during a migraine attack.

Prescription Medications
- Prescription medications are often recommended for severe or frequent migraines.
- Doctors may prescribe triptans, anti-nausea medications, or other migraine-specific drugs.
- These medicines can help reduce symptoms and improve daily functioning.

Acute Treatment Strategies
- Acute treatment focuses on stopping symptoms after they begin.
- People are often advised to rest in a quiet, dark room, stay hydrated, and take medication as soon as symptoms appear.
- Early treatment may reduce the severity and duration of attacks.

Prevention Medications
- Preventive medications are used to reduce the frequency and intensity of migraine attacks.
- Examples include blood pressure medications, antidepressants, anti-seizure drugs, and CGRP inhibitors.
- Preventive treatment is usually considered for people who have frequent migraines.

Non-drug Therapies
- Non-drug therapies can help manage migraines and improve quality of life.
- Common approaches include relaxation techniques, acupuncture, massage therapy, biofeedback, and cognitive behavioral therapy.
- These methods may help reduce stress and migraine triggers.

Lifestyle Modifications
- Healthy lifestyle habits can help prevent migraines.
- Important habits include getting enough sleep, exercising regularly, eating balanced meals, drinking enough water, and avoiding known triggers such as stress, alcohol, or certain foods.
- Keeping a migraine diary may help identify triggers and patterns.
