Definition of Migraine
- Migraine is a neurological disorder characterized by recurring attacks of moderate to severe headaches.
- The pain is often throbbing or pulsating and commonly affects one side of the head.
- Migraine attacks can last from several hours to several days.
- Symptoms may include nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light, sound, or smells.
- Some people experience aura before the headache begins.

Types of Migraine
- Migraine without aura is the most common type and does not include warning signs.
- Migraine with aura includes temporary neurological symptoms such as flashes of light, blind spots, tingling, or speech difficulties.
- Chronic migraine occurs on 15 or more days per month for at least three months.
- Menstrual migraine is linked to hormonal changes during the menstrual cycle.
- Hemiplegic migraine can cause temporary weakness on one side of the body.
- Retinal migraine causes temporary visual disturbances or vision loss in one eye.
- Abdominal migraine is more common in children and causes abdominal pain with migraine-related symptoms.
- Silent migraine includes aura symptoms without a significant headache.

Prevalence and Statistics
- Migraine is one of the most common neurological disorders worldwide.
- The World Health Organization reports that headache disorders affect about 40% of the global population, with migraine a major contributor to disability.
- Migraine is more common in females than males.
- It often begins during puberty or early adulthood.
- Around 12% of people in the United States experience migraines.
- Migraine is a leading cause of disability-adjusted life years globally.

Key Characteristics
- Moderate to severe throbbing or pulsating head pain.
- Pain often on one side of the head.
- Sensitivity to light and sound.
- Nausea and vomiting.
- Visual disturbances or aura in some people.
- Symptoms that can last from 4 to 72 hours.
- Pain that worsens with physical activity.
- Recurring episodes that can interfere with daily life, work, school, and social activities.
- Migraine attacks may move through prodrome, aura, headache, and postdrome phases.
- Common triggers include stress, hormonal changes, lack of sleep, certain foods, weather changes, bright lights, and strong odors.

# Additional Information
Headache disorders are among the most common neurological disorders worldwide and can affect people of all ages, including children, adolescents, and adults. These disorders include migraine, tension-type headache, cluster headache, and medication-overuse headache. Headaches can significantly affect daily life by causing people to miss school, work, sports, and social activities. In 2021, approximately 3.1 billion people worldwide were affected by headache disorders, with females being affected more frequently than males. In addition, migraine is recognized as one of the leading causes of disability worldwide.
Migraine is not simply a severe headache but a neurological disorder characterized by recurring headache attacks. The pain is often described as throbbing or pulsating and usually occurs on one side of the head. Migraine attacks can last from 4 to 72 hours and are commonly accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light and sound. Some individuals may also experience an “aura” before or during a migraine attack, which can include flashes of light, blind spots, tingling sensations, numbness, or difficulty speaking.
Migraine attacks can be divided into four stages: prodrome, aura, attack, and postdrome. The prodrome stage may involve symptoms such as mood changes, food cravings, neck stiffness, frequent yawning, or increased urination. The aura stage involves temporary neurological disturbances, especially visual symptoms. During the attack stage, patients experience severe headache pain along with other associated symptoms. After the headache subsides, the postdrome stage may cause fatigue, confusion, or weakness.
Several factors can trigger migraines, including stress, hormonal changes, lack of sleep, bright lights, loud noises, strong smells, weather changes, alcohol consumption, skipping meals, and certain foods or medications. Genetics also play an important role, as migraines often run in families. Women are more likely to experience migraines than men, partly due to hormonal influences.
Tension-type headaches differ from migraines because they usually cause pressure or tightness around the head rather than throbbing pain. Cluster headaches are less common but extremely severe and are usually centered around one eye, often accompanied by tearing, redness of the eye, and nasal congestion. Medication-overuse headaches occur as a result of excessive or prolonged use of headache medications.
Migraine can have major social, emotional, and economic impacts. People who frequently experience migraines may have reduced work productivity, difficulties in relationships, and an increased risk of mental health problems such as anxiety and depression. Although there is currently no cure for migraine, treatment can help manage symptoms and reduce the frequency of attacks. Treatments include medications, lifestyle modifications, stress management, adequate sleep, hydration, exercise, and avoiding triggers.
Despite the significant impact of headache disorders on quality of life, many patients remain undiagnosed or untreated because of limited awareness, understanding, and access to healthcare services. The World Health Organization (WHO) recognizes headache disorders as an important global public health issue and continues to support improvements in diagnosis, treatment, research, and prevention strategies worldwide.
